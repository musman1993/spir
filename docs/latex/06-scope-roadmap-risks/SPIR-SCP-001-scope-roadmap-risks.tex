\section{Limitations}
These are the limits a user or a vendor will meet in release \SPIRRelease.

\begin{caution}[Not ready for real data on a public address]
The sign-in is \code{admin} / \code{admin} for both portals and is the same for everyone. QR scans are open by
design. Use real data only on a private address (for example a Codespace with private ports) until Phase 1 is done.
\end{caution}

\subsection{Access and accounts}
\begin{itemize}
  \item One shared supplier account that belongs to one supplier at a time. Several suppliers cannot work in parallel
    without changing the profile.
  \item One shared company account; actions are audited as ``Company admin'', not per person.
  \item No password change, lock-out or session revocation screen; sessions expire after 12 hours.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Data and workflow}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Saves are live at once: there is no review before data reaches a QR scan. Mistakes are corrected by a new
    version; the wrong version stays in the history.
  \item Part number, PO and line cannot be changed after saving; a wrong one means a new shipment and revoking the
    old label.
  \item No deletion of shipments (by design for traceability); no supplier deactivation screen.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{QR labels and scanning}
\begin{itemize}
  \item QR codes contain the web address set in \code{PUBLIC\_APP\_URL}. Labels printed with a Codespace address need a
    GitHub sign-in on the phone and stop working when the Codespace is deleted.
  \item Anyone holding a label (or a photo of it) can see that shipment and its certificates.
  \item The rate limiter lives in each function instance; it is a speed bump, not a guarantee.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Label check (OCR)}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Tesseract.js reads full-frame phone photos poorly (26--35\,\% word confidence in our tests). Good results need a
    tight crop of the label and original-resolution photos; glare and stamps can make a label unresolvable.
  \item Only the \code{RECEIVING\_LABELS v1} profile exists; new label layouts need a profile change.
  \item No automatic deskew or perspective correction.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Operations}
\begin{itemize}
  \item No metrics, alerting, backups policy or tested restore; Neon's built-in point-in-time restore is not configured
    or tested.
  \item The Neon production database had only migration 001 applied at the time of writing.
  \item Credentials pasted during set-up (database password, storage keys, QR key) must be treated as exposed and
    rotated (see risk R-04).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Proposal: PO match check}
\begin{keypoint}[The idea]
When a supplier saves a shipment, SPIR compares it with the company's own purchase order. If anything differs, the
shipment is flagged and the right people are alerted before the parts reach the dock. People only look at the
exceptions, instead of approving every shipment.
\end{keypoint}

\textbf{What would be compared} (one row per field; each gives \emph{match}, \emph{within tolerance} or
\emph{discrepancy}):
{\small\zebra
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}L{3.4cm}Y@{}}
  \thead\hd{Field} & \hd{Rule (to agree)}\\
  PO number and line & Must exist on an open PO for this supplier.\\
  Supplier & Must be the PO's supplier.\\
  Part number and revision & Must equal the PO line.\\
  Quantity & Within the PO line's open quantity, with an agreed over/under-shipment tolerance.\\
  Material / grade & Must equal the PO or drawing requirement, where the PO states one.\\
  Ship or due date & Warn if outside the agreed delivery window.\\
\end{tabularx}}

\textbf{What happens on a discrepancy}: the shipment is marked \emph{discrepancy} in both portals with the comparison
shown side by side; an alert goes to the buyer and the Supplier Quality Engineer; at the dock, the scan shows a clear
warning. Whether a discrepancy should \emph{block} the shipment or only \emph{warn} is an open question.

\textbf{Open questions before building}:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Where the PO data comes from: a CSV export of open POs uploaded by the company (possible in Phase 1), or a
    direct ERP feed (Phase 3).
  \item Which fields are blocking and which only warn; the quantity tolerance.
  \item Who receives the alarm (buyer, Supplier Quality Engineer, receiving lead) and how (in the portal, e-mail).
  \item Who can clear an alarm, and whether clearing needs a reason (it should, for the audit trail).
\end{enumerate}
